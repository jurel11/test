## 1. VAJONT DAM, Italy, 9 October 1963  (type: DAM / FLOOD)
======================================================================
### Headline numbers (with grade + exact source)
- Deaths: official 1,917 [A] - Italian Civil Protection Dept page (https://servizio-nazionale.protezionecivile.gov.it/en/pagina-base/vajont-landslide/) says "1,917 lives, and 400 people were never found". Other counts: 1,919 (Fondazione Vajont timeline, https://fondazionevajont.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/TIMELINE_VAJONT_inglese.pdf, Oct 2024) [B]; 2,056 (ASDSO, https://damfailures.org/case-study/vajont-dam-italy-1963/) [B]; 1,900-2,500 range (Wikipedia). CONTRADICTION: use "about 1,900" (matches the channel's own example title).
- Slide volume: 260 million m3 (Fondazione Vajont timeline; Wikipedia) vs 270 million m3 (Civil Protection page prints "270 m3", an evident typo for million; AGU/other) vs "200-300 million m3" (Boon, Houlsby & Utili, Oxford/Warwick preprint https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/sci/eng/research/grouplist/ground/geo/publications/geotechnique_vajoint_pre-print.pdf). Say "about 260 million m3" or "more than a quarter of a billion". UNVERIFIED to the last million.
- Dam: 262 m (860 ft) high, 3.4 m crest width (Wikipedia; ASDSO says 860 ft, 11 ft crest) [B]; one of the tallest dams in the world.
- Wave overtopped dam by ~250 m; about 50 million m3 of water went over (Fondazione timeline; Wikipedia) [B/C]. Dam "remained almost intact": top ~1 m of masonry washed away, structure stands today (Wikipedia citing sources; Fondazione: "the only human artefact to resist the wave and remains undamaged is ... the dam") [B].
- Slide time: 22:39, 9 Oct 1963; whole mass reached the water in ~20 s, slide over in 45 s (Wikipedia; ASDSO "less than 45 seconds").
- Longarone: ASDSO counts 1,269 dead of ~1,328 at risk (94 %) [B]. 20 technical staff in the dam's control center all died (ASDSO) [B].
- Slide volume "about twice the volume of water impounded" (ASDSO) [B].
### Primary sources (named)
- Hendron & Patton (1985) "The Vaiont Slide, a geotechnical analysis based on new geologic observations of the failure surface", US Army Corps of Engineers Technical Report GL-85-5 (NOT GL-85-8), via NTIS - not freely online (per search). [A]
- Semenza & Ghirotti (2000) "History of the 1963 Vaiont slide: the importance of geological factors", Bull. Eng. Geol. Env. 59:87-97 [A] (paywalled; not fetched).
- Muller, L. (1964) "The rock slide in the Vajont valley", Rock Mechanics & Engineering Geology 2 [A].
- Fondazione Vajont, "Timeline of a disaster" (quotes letters/trial documents) [B]. ASDSO case study [B]. Italian Civil Protection page [A].
### Legal/age filter
PASS. 1963 (63 yrs). Criminal: Cassation March 1971 (Biadene 5 yrs incl. 3 condoned; Sensidoni 3 yrs 8 m incl. 3 condoned) - Fondazione timeline. Civil: 1997 Belluno court order vs Montedison; final settlement Montedison-Longarone signed 23 June 1999 (Fondazione timeline). No ongoing case found. Politics: only regulatory facts (dam nationalized to ENEL 1962; test commission).
### Most striking VERIFIED moments/objects
a) Dam stands, town below gone: the dam is intact/standing today; wave ~250 m OVER the crest (documented). Size contrast: slide ~ twice the water in the lake (ASDSO). SAFE to draw: a huge curved wall with a wave pouring over, tiny village far below (no bodies, no people). Do NOT draw the wave hitting houses in detail with people.
b) Lake level yo-yo: filling caused creep to speed up, drawdown slowed it (ASDSO: 0.5 in/day Nov 1962; ceased when lowered Dec 1962-Mar 1963; 0.2 in/day after raising Apr-Jul 1963; >1.2 in/day summer 1963; ~8 in/day by 4 Oct). They were lowering the lake by 1 m/day on 9 Oct (Biadene letter, Fondazione timeline). (Wikipedia gives 25 cm/day in Sept and 1 m/day day-before - conflicts with ASDSO; use ASDSO or say "accelerating".)
c) 9 Oct 12:00: workers on the dam crest see the mountain moving "visible to the naked eye"; 13:00 a crack 50 cm wide, 5 m long opens behind the workers' lodgings (Fondazione timeline, from trial docs). Good 'nothing looked wrong / everybody looked' moment.
d) Model test at Nove (1961-62): gravel slid down a plank into a scale model; conclusion: if lake ~25 m below crest the wave would not overtop (Wikipedia; Fondazione). The model assumed a slide of ~20-40 million m3 (Fondazione line 325) - reality ~260 million: the wrong-by-a-factor-of-~10 number. (Check exact wording before scripting.)
e) Name: "Toc" abbreviated from Friulian "patoc" = "rotten"/"soggy" (Wikipedia Monte Toc) [C]; locals' nickname "the mountain that walks" (ASDSO) [B].

Depict: dam wall + wave over crest; gravel-and-plank model; a crack in the ground; a hammer on rock. Inferred (do not present as seen): what the mountain looked like moving; the inside of the slip surface (clay layers - documented in geologic reports, but drawing is an illustration).

"Why did 1,900 people die under a dam that never broke?" (channel's own example). Must NOT promise a conspiracy or "cover-up" beyond the trial record.

## 2. TACOMA NARROWS BRIDGE, Washington, USA, 7 November 1940  (type: SUSPENSION BRIDGE)
======================================================================
### Headline numbers
- Opened 1 July 1940; collapsed 7 Nov 1940: 4 months. Third-longest suspension span in the world at opening (WSDOT home). [A - WSDOT official history site https://wsdot.wa.gov/tnbhistory/]
- Only victim: "Tubby", a black cocker spaniel belonging to Leonard Coatsworth's daughter (WSDOT https://wsdot.wa.gov/tnbhistory/tubby-trivia.htm). No people died. [A]
- Timeline (WSDOT https://wsdot.wa.gov/tnbhistory/bridge-timeline.htm): wind 38 mph at 07:30, 42 mph at 09:30; 10:03 twisting begins; by 10:07 roadway tilting up to 28 ft on one side then the other, angle up to 45 degrees; 10:30 first concrete falls; 11:02 a 600-ft section falls; 11:08 final section falls. [A]
- Deck: 8-ft-deep solid plate girders; width-to-span ratio 1:72 (WSDOT https://wsdot.wa.gov/tnbhistory/bridges-failure.htm). [A]
- Cost: $6.4 million budget; insured for $5.2 M (80 %) across 22 companies; State-insurers settlement $4 million (Aug 1941) (WSDOT aftermath page). [A]
- Design history: Eldridge's design had a 25-ft-deep stiffening truss at an $11 M estimate; PWA would approve $7 M; Moisseiff's slender design with 8-ft plate girders was used (Eldridge autobiography as quoted by WSDOT aftermath page) [B - one participant's account]. PWA's own field engineer David L. Glenn refused to recommend acceptance in July 1940 (WSDOT aftermath) [A].
- Bounce: "riveters and other workmen notice the 'bounce' or 'galloping'" in May 1940, some "chew on lemons to combat nausea"; four hydraulic jacks installed as shock absorbers had no effect; at opening "Engineers announce that there is nothing dangerous in the bridge's bounce"; bounce in winds as light as 4 mph (WSDOT timeline) [A].
- Pivotal event: slipping of the cable band at mid-span on the north cable, changing motion from vertical to twisting (Carmody Board, WSDOT). [A]
### Primary sources
- Federal Works Agency (1941) "The Failure of the Tacoma Narrows Bridge" (Ammann, von Karman, Woodruff = "Carmody Board" report), HathiTrust https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015021064251 [A].
- WSDOT Tacoma Narrows Bridge history site [A]. Billah & Scanlan (1991) Am. J. Phys. 59:118 (resonance myth) [A, not yet fetched].
### MYTH to call out
The textbook "resonance / Karman vortex" explanation is not the accepted cause; WSDOT itself says the cause "remains a mystery... primary explanation is torsional flutter" (self-excited aeroelastic motion). Do not say "the wind matched the bridge's natural frequency". (Confirm with Billah & Scanlan.)
### Legal/age filter
PASS. 1940; insurance settled Aug 1941 (WSDOT). Insurance agent Hallet French arrested Dec 1940 for pocketing premiums for an $800,000 policy (trial 1941) - closed, and irrelevant to thumbnail.
### Most striking VERIFIED moments/objects
a) Solid steel side wall (8-ft plate girder) of the deck - passengers "saw water on one side and then the other" (Richard Swinney recalled "The sides of the bridge were solid walls", WSDOT weird facts) [B]. Depict: roadway tilted like a seesaw with a solid green wall on the side; car (empty) sliding.
b) 4 months of visible warning, "Galloping Gertie" was a tourist attraction and engineers said the bounce wasn't dangerous - 'nothing looked wrong' moment.
c) Lemon-chewing workmen (WSDOT timeline) - real, odd, drawable.
d) The last car: Coatsworth abandoned his car ~450 ft past the East Tower with Tubby inside; Prof. Farquharson tried to rescue Tubby and was bitten (WSDOT Tubby trivia). AVOID depicting the dog's death; the fact can be told in words.
e) Film: Barney Elliott (camera shop owner) + Farquharson filmed; last man on the bridge = Farquharson (WSDOT weird facts).
### Depiction guidance
Documented: roadway twisting, tilt up to 28 ft each side / 45 deg, solid girders, green color ("Narrows Green" - the same color as later bridges, WSDOT). Inferred: nothing else needed.
### Title + honest promise
"Why did a brand-new bridge twist itself apart in a light wind?" (wind 42 mph is "light" only by comparison with the 100+ mph design assumption; check the design-wind figure before scripting - NOT verified in my sources.) Promise: torsional flutter explained; cost-saving slender design; the four months of warning; "the only casualty was a dog" fact.
### Planted object
A lemon (workers chewed lemons for nausea) - yellow, palette-friendly; or the mid-span cable band that slipped.

## 3. HYATT REGENCY WALKWAYS, Kansas City, USA, 17 July 1981  (type: BUILDING / WALKWAY)
======================================================================
### Headline numbers - PRIMARY: NBS report
- NBS/NIST "Investigation of the Kansas City Hyatt Regency Walkways Collapse", NBSIR 82-2465 (Marshall, Pfrang, Leyendecker, Woodward et al., Feb 1982), PDF https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/IR/nbsir82-2465.pdf (downloaded; text extracted in raw/hyatt_nist.txt). [A]
- 17 July 1981, ~7:05 p.m.; 113 dead, 186 injured (NBS Executive Summary). Many other sources say 114 dead / 216 injured: CONTRADICTION - use NBS figures or "more than 110". Crowd in atrium at 7:00 pm estimated 1,500-2,000 (NBS timeline); 63 people estimated on walkways (from TV video analysis).
- "Most devastating structural collapse ever to take place in the United States" (NBS, 1982; later superseded by 9/11 and other events - say "at the time").
- Two suspended walkways (2nd, 4th floors) hung from atrium roof; the 2nd-floor walkway hung from the 4th-floor one.
- Design change: contract drawings = one continuous hanger rod through 4th and 2nd floor box beams; as built = two sets of rods (roof->4th floor beams; 4th->2nd floor beams) => change "essentially doubled the load" on the 4th-floor box beam-hanger rod connection. Shop drawings carried review stamps of contractor, structural engineer and architect (NBS Exec Summary).
- Capacity: at collapse the load on a 4th-floor connection was 31 % of the ultimate capacity a code-compliant connection should have; even the ORIGINAL continuous-rod design would have had ~60 % of code capacity - so it was under-designed from the start; as-built walkways had "only minimal capacity to resist their own weight" from the day of construction (NBS Abstract).
- Hanger rods 1 1/4 in (32 mm) diameter; box beams = pair of 8-in steel channels welded toe to toe, resting on washers and nuts (NBS). Neither workmanship nor materials played a significant role (NBS conclusion 9).
### Legal/age filter
PASS. 1981. Missouri Board of Architects, Engineers & Land Surveyors: complaint Feb 1984; 1985 Administrative Hearing Commission (442-page decision) found Duncan and Gillum (and G.C.E. International) guilty of gross negligence/misconduct; upheld by Missouri Court of Appeals 1988 (Duncan v. Missouri Bd. for Architects, https://law.justia.com/cases/missouri/court-of-appeals/1988/52655-0.html). Civil settlements > $140 M reported by secondary sources (UNVERIFIED primary). No ongoing case. CHANNEL RULE: never blame individuals - tell it as a change-order/communication system failure ("who owns the change?").
### Most striking VERIFIED moments/objects
a) The single change: "one long rod" vs "two shorter rods" - depicted as a physical diagram-free cutaway: rod through a box beam, nut and washer under the beam. Safe to draw (documented in NBS). The 'nut and washer' sitting on a welded seam: documented as the failure initiation at the 4th-floor middle box beam east end (location 9UE).
b) The number: 31 % - NBS. And even the original was ~60 %.
c) Video: a TV crew filmed the walkways minutes before (NBS) - the source for "63 people".
### Depiction guidance
Draw the atrium with the two hanging walkways intact (before) and a lone rod + nut; NO crowd under the walkways in the collapse frame. Inferred: exact sequence of the collapse (NBS says failure "most probably" began at 9UE).
### Title + honest promise
"Why did a hotel walkway built to hold 100 people fall with 63 on it?" - CAUTION: 'built to hold 100' is NOT verified; do not use. Safer: "Why did two hotel walkways fall from a ceiling that a single change had just weakened?" or simpler: "Why did a tiny change to one steel rod bring down two hotel walkways?" Promise: the change from one rod to two; the code shortfall in the ORIGINAL design (60 %); who reviewed the shop drawings.
### Planted object
A nut and washer on a hanger rod (documented; exact hardware: 1 1/4 in heavy hex nut, NBS).

## 4. DE HAVILLAND COMET, 1954  (type: AIRCRAFT)
======================================================================
### Headline numbers
- BOAC Flight 781, G-ALYP, 10 Jan 1954, near Elba: all 35 aboard died. SAA Flight 201, G-ALYY, 8 April 1954, near Naples: all 21 died (Aerossurance https://aerossurance.com/safety-management/comet-misconceptions/ ; Wikipedia). NOTE Withey's paper prints "8 January 1954" - a typo; the inquiry title says 10 January.
- First jet airliner in service: 2 May 1952 (Withey 1997).
- Court of Inquiry under Lord Cohen: report published 1 Feb 1955 as Civil Aircraft Accident Report (CAP 127), HMSO. Official copy at https://asn.flightsafety.org/reports/1954/19540408_COMT_G-ALYY.pdf (TLS failure from this environment - NOT read; used Withey 1997 + Aerossurance + Wikipedia which cite it). [A for existence; content via B/C]
- Royal Navy recovered ~70 % by weight of main structure of G-ALYP (Wikipedia; Withey; Aerossurance) from the sea bed; the wreck was rebuilt at Farnborough; first use of underwater TV for such a recovery (Withey).
- Water tank test at RAE Farnborough of an identical airliner G-ALYU: whole fuselage in a tank, pressurized with water, wings out through seals; "flight" = 5 min; failed after 3,057 cycles (1,221 real + 1,836 simulated) [Withey; Wikipedia] or 3,060 (1,230 + 1,830) [Aerossurance]. Crack < 2 mm at final failure; started at a bolt/rivet hole near the forward escape hatch (Withey). Date 24 June 1954 (Wikipedia).
- G-ALYP had made about 1,286 pressurized flights (Withey: 1,286 + 255 unpressurized) / 1,290 (Wikipedia) when it broke up; G-ALYY 900. Say "about 1,300 flights".
- Origin of the Elba failure: fatigue crack from a bolt/rivet hole at the rear corner of the rear ADF (radio compass) 'window' cut-out in the cabin roof (Withey; Wikipedia). 
### MYTH
"Square windows caused it." Wikipedia (citing Cohen report) and Withey: Comet 1 windows were rectangles with rounded corners; failures started from ADF antenna cut-outs / rivet-bolt holes near cut-outs, not passenger window shape; Withey says oval windows on later marks were about being able to use Redux glue, not about squareness. Aerossurance agrees ("misconception 2"). Grade: A/B/C mix; the Cohen report itself not read.
### Legal/age filter
PASS. 1954-55; report published 1955; no litigation. Note: G-ALYV (Calcutta, May 1953, 43 dead) was a separate storm-related break-up; separate inquiry.
### Most striking VERIFIED moments/objects
a) An entire airliner sunk in a purpose-built water tank at Farnborough and "flown" thousands of times faster than real life (5 minutes per flight). Cartoon-safe; no people; is documented as a photograph (aerial view of G-ALYU in tank, Crown Copyright, in Withey Fig. 2).
b) The wreck of G-ALYP dredged from the sea bed and rebuilt on a frame at Farnborough (first use of underwater TV).
c) The crack: less than 2 mm when the test fuselage failed (Withey) - "a crack you could hide under a coin". (Illustrate; don't invent measurement of the real Elba crack, which was not observed in flight.)
d) Why testing missed it: the earlier fatigue test used the same fuselage that had been proof-pressure tested and cold-worked -> improved fatigue life, masked the weakness (Aerossurance quoting; Court of Inquiry). [B/C]
### Title + honest promise
"Why did the first jet airliner break apart at 35,000 feet - twice?" (G-ALYY at 35,000 ft per Withey; G-ALYP was climbing through 27,000 ft per Withey; use 'in the sky' or 'over the Mediterranean'.) Promise: fatigue; the tank test; the square-window myth debunked; the wreck rebuilt from the sea.
### Planted object
A single rivet (bolt hole) or a cabin window; alternatively the water tank.

## 10. SAMSUNG GALAXY NOTE 7, worldwide, Aug-Oct 2016  (type: FAILED PRODUCT)
======================================================================
Sources: Wikipedia (cites Samsung's 23 Jan 2017 findings, UL, Exponent, TUV Rheinland; FAA/DOT) [C]; Samsung release 23 Jan 2017 [B] not fetched; CPSC recall page fetch FAILED.
