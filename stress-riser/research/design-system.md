## 1. Palette maths (script: palette_math.py; validated against reference values)
Validation: APCA implementation reproduces published Lc 106.04 (#000 on #fff), -107.88, 63.06 (#888 on #fff); dE2000 reproduces Sharma et al. test pair 2.0425. CVD = Machado, Oliveira, Fernandes 2009 matrices, severity 1.0, applied in linear RGB [A: doi.org/10.1109/tvcg.2009.113; matrices from the paper as implemented in colorspace/R and many tools].
Single colours (CIELAB D65): ink L*9.3 | white 100 | paper 93.0 | sky 87.7 | grn_l 72.1 | grn_d 47.8 | brown 49.2 | tan 75.0 | amber 75.4 | red 51.8. Chroma: red 68.6, amber 65.4, grn_l 56.0, grn_d 42.7 (all others < 35).
KEY LIGHTNESS CLUSTERS: three tiers only - LIGHT (white 100, paper 93, sky 88, amber 75, tan 75, grn_l 72), MID (red 52, brown 49, grn_d 48), DARK (ink 9). Colours inside a tier are NOT separable by lightness (grayscale/colour-blind/blur), only by hue.
Thresholds used: WCAG 2.x text 4.5:1 (large 3:1), non-text 3:1 [A: w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html]; Material HCT: tone(L*) difference 40 => contrast >= 3.0, difference 50 => >= 4.5 [A: material-color-utilities hct.ts lines 29-31, I read the source]; APCA levels Lc 90 preferred body, 75 min body, 60 min fluent text, 45 min large text, 30 min any text, 15 invisibility [A: Myndex/SAPC-APCA documentation/minimum_compliance.md, read raw].
Practical rule I derived: dL* >= 50 = text-safe; 40-50 = big bold text or shape only; 25-40 = big flat shapes only, needs ink outline; < 25 = luminance-invisible, relies on hue + outline.

Key pairs (WCAG / dL* / dE2000 / APCA Lc / dE2000 protan-deutan-tritan) - full 45-pair table in palette_report.md:
- ink/white 17.4 / 90.7 / 86 / 104; ink/paper 14.6 / 83.7 / 84 / 92; ink/sky 12.7 / 78.4 / 79 / 84; ink/amber 8.95 / 66.2 / 66 / 65; ink/tan 8.8 / 65.7 / 62 / 64; ink/grn_l 8.1 / 62.9 / 61 / 60.
- white/grn_d 4.85 / 52.2 / 45 / -79; white/brown 4.6 / 50.8 / 42 / -77; white/red 4.2 / 48.2 / 44 / -73.5; paper/grn_d 4.06 / 45.2 / 40 / -66; paper/brown 3.86 / 43.7; paper/red 3.5 / 41.2 / 41 / -60.
- ink/red 4.13 / 42.5 / 43 / 34 (WCAG passes but Lc only 34: ink text on red FAILS at small size; ink OUTLINE on red is fine: 3:1 non-text). ink/brown 3.8 / 40.0; ink/grn_d 3.6 / 38.5.
- red/sky 3.06 / 35.9 / 52 / -51 (hue difference big, lightness ok): red object on sky is a strong pop; red/paper 3.5 / 41.
- amber/red 2.16 / 23.7 / 38 (deutan dE 17.7, tritan 23) - the two accents must never touch.
FAIL / DANGER pairs (dL* < 25): tan/amber 0.5 (!), grn_d/brown 1.4, brown/red 2.5, grn_l/tan 2.9, grn_l/amber 3.3, grn_d/red 4.0 (protan dE 5.5, deutan 10.9: near-identical for ~8 % of men), paper/sky 5.3 (WCAG 1.15), white/paper 7.0 (1.2), sky/white 12.3 (1.37), sky/amber 12.2, sky/grn_l 15.6, sky/tan 12.7, paper/tan 18.0, paper/amber 17.5, grn_l/red 20.4 (deutan dE 13).
Colour-blind: NEI: ~8 % of men (1 in 12), 0.5 % of women of Northern European descent have red-green CVD [A/B: NEI as quoted by several pages; could not open NEI page itself]. After simulation red (#d94a38) becomes #726835 (protan) / #968733 (deutan) = same olive as dark green (#817434 / #7a703e): red-on-green scenes lose the accent for these viewers. Pairs that stay >= 30 dE2000 in ALL three simulations: everything involving ink or white with any other colour except white/paper, white/sky, white/tan, paper/tan; red/sky (39.7-56.9), amber/sky (35.7-41.2), red/paper (31-40), red/white, red/tan (>=17.9 deutan - only marginal), sky/grn_d (32-42), sky/brown (39-50).
Accent choice: red #d94a38 vs amber #e6b23a. Computed chroma equal (68.6 vs 65.4) but red is mid-lightness (L*52) and amber light (L*75). Frequency-tuned saliency proxy (Achanta 2009; 168x94 downscale; 40 random cluttered palette scenes per cell; experiments.py): accent disc is the most salient point in 95 % of scenes for red (mean over 8 backgrounds) vs 69 % for amber; red >= 100 % on paper, sky, grn_l, grn_d, tan, white; weaker on brown (85 %) and ink (78 %). Amber fails on grn_l (2 %), brown (8 %), grn_d (48 %) but wins on ink (100 %) and works on paper/sky/tan/white (98-100 %). [computational model = C-grade evidence, but consistent with lightness/hue logic.] CAVEAT: FT saliency uses Lab distance (chroma-heavy), so 'amber on tan 98 %' is misleading: amber/tan has dL* 0.5 and tritan dE2000 7.7 - treat as FAIL in grayscale/CVD; likewise amber on white/paper works by hue only (dL* 25/17.5; min-CVD dE 28/21). Amber on ink is the one strong amber pairing (dL 66, min-CVD dE 62). Red on dark backgrounds: red/ink dL 42.5, min-CVD 31 OK. Red on brown (dL 2.5, protan dE 2.9) and dark green (dL 4.0, protan 5.5) = FAIL for luminance/CVD even though the proxy still found it 85-100 %.
Rule: RED is the default single accent; AMBER only on ink/very dark scenes (night, reactor, tunnel) or paper/sky; never amber on green/brown; never red on brown/dark-green without an ink outline AND a paper-coloured halo/adjacent light area.

## 3. Text overlay
Vision-science bounds [A]:
- Ohnishi & Oda 2020, i-Perception 11(6) (doi 10.1177/2041669520981102, PMC7768324): legibility plateaus at ~12 samples (pixels) per letter height, 6 spl still readable but at higher contrast threshold [via WebFetch summary; raw page blocked by captcha].
- ISO 9241-303 / ANSI-HFES 100: minimum Latin character height 16 arc-min, displays should support 20-22 arc-min [via search summaries of the standards, not the standards themselves - B].
- Bababekova et al. 2011, Optom Vis Sci 88(7):795-797: mean smartphone viewing distance 36.2 cm texting, 32.2 cm web, n=129 (age 18-39) [A].
My calculation: phone css px ~0.166 mm (iPhone 6.1-in 460 ppi / 3 -> 153 ppi; UNVERIFIED spec quote); 12 css px cap height at 36 cm = 19 arc-min (in ISO 20-22 preferred band); 9.6 px = 16 arc-min.
=> Required cap height in MASTER pixels (1280 wide) = displayed cap px x 1280/display width:
   12 px (comfortable, 19 arc-min) -> 168 wide: 91 px | 246: 62 | 310: 50 | 360: 43 | 390: 39 | 414: 37
   ~7 px (bare minimum, OCR proxy) -> 168: 53 | 246: 36 | 360: 25
Machine proxy (RapidOCR, white fill + ink outline 12 % of cap on striped palette background, downscaled to 168x94 then 3x bicubic; 6 test words WHY/FELL/BROKE/WHO?/CRACK/SNAP; ocr_log.txt): 'WHO?' (question mark) is usually misread so 5 = full score. At 168x94 the first master cap height from which every larger size scores >= 4/6: Fredoka 700, Baloo 2 800, Rubik 900 = 40 px (5.2 px displayed); Lilita One, Titan One, Paytone One, Bowlby One, Londrina Solid Black, Comic Neue Bold, League Gothic, Permanent Marker, Patrick Hand, Luckiest Guy = 50 px (6.6 px displayed); Bangers 60 px (7.9); Anton 70 px (9.2); Passion One Black never stable (letters merge, even at 120). At 246x138: 30 px (5.8 px displayed) for most rounded fonts. Lesson: with a 12 % outside stroke, condensed low-stem fonts (Anton stem/cap 0.20, Bangers 0.20, Passion One) lose counters and need >= 60-70 px cap - scale outline to the font (about 8 % of cap for condensed, 12 % for rounded heavy). The machine floor (~5-7 px displayed cap height) matches the 6-samples-per-letter lower end of Ohnishi & Oda; human comfort (12 px) needs ~91 master px at 168 wide. [C-grade proxy for relative ranking only, not human data.]
RECOMMENDATION (1280x720): main word cap height 100-140 px (min 90), secondary min 60 px, outline (stroke outside, round joins) 12 % of cap = 12-16 px in ink #1a1a1a, plus optional hard 6-8 px offset ink shadow (no blur: blur shadows vanish at 168 px). 1-3 words, <= 16 characters per line, max 2 lines (brief says 1-4 words); Lilita One at cap 100 fits ~16 characters on 1152 px; Anton at cap 100 ~25 characters. Font size = cap / cap-em ratio (Lilita 0.70 -> 143 px; Titan 0.71 -> 141; Anton 0.86 -> 116; Bangers 0.742 -> 135).
Text colours: white or paper fill + ink outline works on every palette background because ink vs white dL 90.7. Amber fill (ink outline) ok (dL 66). Never red/green/brown as text fill. Ink text needs a light background (white/paper/sky/tan/amber/grn_l; Lc 60-92) - NOT red/brown/grn_d (Lc 29-34). On red/brown/dark-green areas, sit the text on a paper caption box with ink outline, or use white fill + thicker outline.
Placement: outer margin 64 px left/right, 36 px top/bottom (rounded corners 8-12 css px = ~43-61 master px), keep-out bottom-right 230x90, keep-out top-right ~110x110 (desktop hover icons, UNVERIFIED), text block in the upper-left or left third; do not put the only readable word on the extreme right edge (feed truncation is not an issue for the image, but 3-dot menu on mobile sits beside the title, not on the thumbnail).
Font classes (all free, licences read from google/fonts METADATA.pb: OFL unless noted): [A] Class 1 rounded heavy display (best cartoon match and best small-size performance): Lilita One (OFL; cap/em 0.70; stem/cap 0.28), Titan One (OFL; stem 0.39, wide), Paytone One (OFL), Sigmar One (OFL; stem 0.53, very wide: only 1-2 words), Coiny, Sniglet ExtraBold, Bowlby One. Class 2 comic caps: Luckiest Guy (Apache-2.0; x/cap 0.97, all-caps look, tight spacing), Bangers (OFL; condensed 7.2 cap-widths per 16 chars - fits large, but stem/cap only 0.20 so thin at 168), Chewy (Apache). Class 3 condensed grotesque for numbers: Anton (0.86 cap/em: biggest cap per font size), League Gothic, Oswald 700, Bebas Neue: use only for the number in 'title-like' statements ('1,900'), stem/cap 0.15-0.22 = weak below cap 90. Class 4 hand-lettered: Londrina Solid Black (0.29), Permanent Marker (0.21), Gluten 900, Grandstander 900; handwriting fonts Patrick Hand / Kalam / Comic Neue are too thin (stem/cap 0.12-0.19) for 168 px - only for secondary line at large cap. Pick ONE face for the series: Lilita One (primary); Titan One or Luckiest Guy as alternate style voices only if the series rules allow.

## 4. Series consistency
Evidence:
- Distinctive brand assets (Romaniuk, Building Distinctive Brand Assets, 2018; Ehrenberg-Bass): assets become useful when famous (recognised) AND unique; characters/mascots, colours, shapes are the main types; needs consistent repetition over time [B; via ANA/creativeX/Zappi summaries]. A 2026 Int. J. Advertising paper 'Shape-based assets are strongest' (tandfonline 10.1080/02650487.2026.2637295) - I could only see its title/snippet (403): UNVERIFIED details; if true it supports the stick figure silhouette (shape) as the strongest anchor rather than colour.
- Banner blindness (Benway & Lane 1998; NN/g 1997/2007/2018 eyetracking) [A: nngroup.com/articles/banner-blindness-old-and-new-findings]: people ignore things that LOOK LIKE ADS (position, animation/fancy formatting, proximity to ads); example 0.8 % of attention in right rail on 25 % of area. It is NOT evidence that a consistent channel template is ignored. Myth check: 'consistent templates cause banner blindness' is an extrapolation; the risk is the thumbnail looking like an ad/branded banner (logo bar, ribbon, boxed frame) or repetition making every video look 'already seen'.
- Pop-out is feature contrast vs the surround, saturating at some distance and vanishing when feature difference is small (Nothdurft 1993; Wolfe & Horowitz 2017 Nature Hum Behav 'Five factors that guide attention', doi 10.1038/s41562-017-0058) [A]. Implication: ONE accent colour object against calmer surround; a template that puts the same accent in the same place is useful for recognition, but if every thumbnail is equally loud nothing pops.
- CTR-improvement claims for consistency ('38 % higher CTR', '15 % higher retention', '70/30 rule') come from SEO blogs without method [C] - UNVERIFIED, do not quote.
- Paddy Galloway: same style/branding across titles and thumbnails, <= 3 elements, bright [C via summaries].
- YouTube Test & Compare (up to 3 variants, watch-time share) is the only first-party way to measure this [A].

## 5. Production workflow
Docs [A]:
- OpenAI image models (developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation, Sept 2026): current names gpt-image-2.5-sunburst (editing precision) / gpt-image-2.5-flare (fast); sizes any WxH multiples of 16, aspect 1:3-3:1, max edge 3840; quality low..max; PNG/JPEG/WebP with output_compression; background:'transparent' for PNG/WebP; edit endpoint takes multiple reference images and a mask (must have alpha, same size/format, < 50 MB). Documented limitations: text placement/clarity, consistency of recurring characters, precise placement. Cookbook prompt guide: order background/scene -> subject -> key details -> constraints, state intended use, 'change only X, keep everything else the same', re-specify critical details if they drift, results more variable above 2560x1440.
- Google Nano Banana (cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-nano-banana): formula [Subject]+[Action]+[Location]+[Composition]+[Style]; up to 14 reference images for consistency; semantic-mask inpainting by text; 'use positive framing: describe what you want, not what you don't want (e.g. empty street instead of no cars)'; aspect ratios incl. 16:9; 1K/2K/4K.
- Midjourney V7 Omni Reference --oref, weight --ow 1-1000 default 100 (docs.midjourney.com Omni-Reference; page blocked, details via secondary summaries [B/C]); --sref for style.
- FLUX.1 Kontext (docs.bfl.ml/kontext): iterative editing with character preservation, name the subject explicitly ('the stick figure with the round white head'), state what to preserve, change one thing at a time [B/A].
- Negation failure: text-to-image models often ignore 'no X' (arXiv FineGRAIN 2512.02161, NeIn 2409.06481; cosine 0.92 between 'running' and 'not running' embeddings quoted in one paper) [A/B]. => the 'never put text' rule should be backed by positive descriptions ('blank plain wooden board', 'clock face with no marks' , 'gauge dial with a single needle and no marks') and by post-check/inpaint, not only by a negative list.
- Compositing tools: Figma (free plan, stroke outside + round joins, export PNG @1x/3x), Canva (free, Google Fonts), Photopea (free in browser, PSD layer styles), Affinity by Canva (free forever since 30 Oct 2025, Windows/macOS, needs Canva account; generative AI only with paid Canva) [B: cgchannel/macrumors/tidbits Oct 2025], GIMP 3, Krita. Live text with outline is the safest.
Export (measured by me): flat-colour cartoon at 1280x720: PNG-24 33 KB, PNG-8 (64 colours) 14.5 KB, JPEG q90 4:2:0 63 KB, q90 4:4:4 76 KB, q95 4:4:4 94 KB - PNG is SMALLER than JPEG for flat art and avoids a second lossy generation before YouTube's own re-encode; use PNG-24 sRGB (no alpha, no interlace) under 2 MB; if gradients/grain push it above 2 MB use JPEG q92 4:4:4. Chroma 4:2:0 test (mean dE76 in a 16 px band at a hard edge, q90): 1.4-3.1 for 4:2:0 vs 0.0-0.65 for 4:4:4; edge softened by 2 px - small, and YouTube re-encodes to 4:2:0 anyway; matters only for edges without an ink outline (red/dark green worst: dE 3.1, both same lightness).
sRGB: assume sRGB (YouTube serves untagged/converted JPEG? UNVERIFIED whether ICC of the upload is honoured) - export with sRGB IEC61966-2.1 embedded or converted, never Display-P3/Adobe RGB.
Upload master 3840x2160 (official recommendation) only if PNG stays < 2 MB (mobile) - otherwise 1920x1080/1280x720; delivered size is 1280x720 either way (measured).

## 6. Fixed anchors vs variables (proposal; grade C = my design reasoning built on the evidence above)
FIXED (never change): (1) the 10-colour palette only; (2) ink #1a1a1a outline on every shape, 12-16 px on the 1280 master; (3) stick-figure design exactly per character sheet (round white head, dot eyes, line brows/mouth, single-line limbs, one prop), head >= 150 px if the face carries the emotion; (4) ONE accent object per thumbnail, 3-8 % of frame area, in red #d94a38 (amber #e6b23a only on ink-dark scenes), never both; (5) one typeface (Lilita One) in white or paper fill + ink outside stroke (round joins), 1-3 words, cap 100-140 px, caps only; (6) flat colour + gentle cel shading, no gradients/glows/photo textures; (7) safe zones (64/36 px margins, bottom-right 230x90 free); (8) the figure is always the same scale class (one clear subject) and the 'whole illustrated place' rule.
VARIABLES: composition (close-up figure / wide place with tiny figure / hero object fills frame / diagonal break / low-angle / cutaway), hero object, dominant background family (paper, sky+green, brown/tan interior, ink night, dark green), emotion, text position within the upper-left/left-third zone (or lower-left), word count 1-3, text box vs free text, degree of cutaway, camera distance.
Rule of thumb: keep ~5 anchors identical and vary ~4 axes per video; consecutive thumbnails should differ in dominant background colour family (use the L* tiers: light / mid / dark) so a feed of your videos alternates rather than repeats.

## 7. Per-thumbnail QA checklist (run tools/thumb_preview.py <img> --title "<title>" --duration 10:24 --safezone)
1 squint: blur column - accent object + main word + figure silhouette still visible. 2 phone size: read the 360 and 168 rows at 100 % zoom without leaning in; main word cap >= 90 px on master (>= 12 px displayed at 168). 3 grayscale column: word vs its background dL* >= 50, figure vs background >= 25, accent vs surround >= 25 (else add ink outline/halo). 4 light AND dark rows: image edge must not dissolve on white (avoid pale sky/paper/white along all four edges) . 5 CVD: accent never red-on-green/brown, amber never on tan/light green. 6 badge/progress-bar zone empty (230x90 bottom-right). 7 AI-glyph check at 100 %: no letters/numbers/marks on signs, gauges, clocks, papers, book spines, rope textures; no fingers/shoes/thick limbs/smile unless asked. 8 word count <= 3 (max 4), no title repetition. 9 export: 16:9, >= 1280x720 (ideally 1920x1080 or 3840x2160 if < 2 MB), sRGB, PNG-24 (or JPEG q92 4:4:4), < 2 MB, no alpha. 10 after publish: YouTube Studio Test & Compare with up to 3 variants (watch-time-share winner).

## 8. Prompt skeleton (tool-agnostic; built from OpenAI order scene->subject->details->constraints and Google formula, positive framing)
"Hand-drawn cartoon illustration, clean black outlines, soft flat colors with gentle cel shading, warm natural light; palette limited to #1a1a1a #ffffff #f3ead8 #bfe2ea #8fbf5a #4f7d3a #9a6b43 #d2b48c #e6b23a #d94a38. Wide 16:9 thumbnail composition for a phone screen: ONE big hero shape [the cracked dam wall] filling the right two-thirds; the stick figure from the attached character sheet (round white head with black outline, two dot eyes, line eyebrows and mouth showing [worried], single-line arms and legs, holding [a clipboard]) in the lower-left third, large in frame; the upper-left third is a calm, plain [sky-colored] area with nothing in it; a single [red #d94a38] object in the hero shape. Signs and gauges are blank plain shapes. Fully flat, bold simple shapes, little background detail." Then edit passes: "change only X; keep everything else identical".
Text/numbers are added afterwards as a live-text layer in Figma/Canva/Photopea/Affinity/GIMP.
