## 1. Specs — YouTube Help "Add custom thumbnails on YouTube" (fetched raw 2026-09-30)
URL: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431?hl=en  [A]
- Custom thumbs: "Custom video and Shorts thumbnails ... upload your own if your account is verified". Custom Shorts thumbnails only in YouTube Studio on computer (as of fetch).
- Add custom Short thumbnail: Studio > Content > Shorts > Thumbnail > Upload file; "For uploaded files, the recommended aspect ratio is 9:16."
- Recommended: "resolution of 3840 x 2160 pixels for videos and 2160 x 3840 for Shorts, with a minimum width of 640 pixels for videos and a minimum height of 640 pixels for Shorts." Formats "such as JPG or PNG".
- Size limits by upload device: Mobile: 2 MB for video thumbnails (10 MB podcasts). Desktop: 50MB for video, Shorts, podcast thumbnails.
- Aspect: videos 16:9; Shorts 9:16; podcast playlists 1:1.
- NOTE (verbatim): "Vertical videos with 16:9 custom thumbnails will be replaced by an auto-generated 4:5 thumbnail on the home, explore, and subscription pages. Your custom thumbnail will still appear on the watch feed, watch history, and non-mobile platforms."
- Thumbnail policy: may be rejected + strike if contains Nudity/sexually provocative; Hate speech; Violence; Harmful or dangerous content. Repeat: removal of custom thumbnail privileges for 30 days or termination.
- Daily custom thumbnail limit exists; varies by country/channel history.
- "YouTube may turn off custom thumbnails for certain search results when they're considered inappropriate for viewers."
- Ask Studio can generate thumbnail suggestions (uses transcript/themes).
=> OLD SPEC (1280x720, <2MB, min width 640) is out of date on official page; still widely quoted by 3rd-party sites (e.g. thumbnailtest.com, snappa). Contradiction/myth to flag.

## 3. A/B — YouTube Help "A/B test titles & thumbnails" (answer 13861714 / 16391400), fetched raw 2026-09-30 [A]
- Up to 3 titles and/or thumbnails; options: Title only, Thumbnail only, Title and thumbnail.
- Desktop only (YouTube Studio); need "advanced features" enabled.
- Not for Shorts, Scheduled Lives, Premieres (eligible after Premiere ends + converts); Live Archives OK. Not for made-for-kids, mature audiences, or private videos. Age-restricted: test won't run; uploading age-restricted content to tool without marking = lose feature access.
- If title or thumbnail changed during the test, test stops automatically.
- "Your test should be completed within two weeks." "a few days or up to 2 weeks ... due to differences in impressions, how recently your video was published".
- Winner = "highest watch time" shown to all viewers. If no clear winner, FIRST uploaded option becomes default. Results: Winner / Performed Same / Inconclusive "based on watch time share".
- Control group: "We may maintain a small percentage of traffic as a control group ... only see the default title and thumbnail. ... excluded from experiment calculations."
- Why watch time: "Great titles and thumbnails serve an important purpose beyond getting viewers to click. They help a viewer understand what the video is about so that they don't waste their time clicking on the wrong videos. ... we optimize tests for overall watch time over other metrics, like click-through-rate."
- Res: "If the resolution of any thumbnail is lower than 720p (1280 x 720), all experiment thumbnails will be downscaled to 480p (854 x 480)."
- Tips: test older videos first; run diverse tests (too-similar variants run longer); give time; upload high-res.
- 3rd-party tools "run A/B tests sequentially ... often only optimize for click-through rate".
- Early impressions from existing fans; later from new viewers -> results vary.
- "Not enough impressions" -> no winner.

## 2. Impressions & CTR — Help "Impressions & click-through-rate FAQs" (answer 7628154) + "Check your YouTube thumbnail impressions and watch time" (9314486), fetched raw 2026-09-30 [A]
- Registered impression: "shown for more than 1 second and at least 50% of the thumbnail is visible on the screen."
- Counted on: YouTube on computers, TVs, game consoles, Android, iPhone, iPad; Search; homepage (incl autoplay); feeds (subs, trending, history, Watch Later); Up Next (incl autoplay); Playlists.
- NOT counted: external websites/apps; YouTube mobile website; YouTube Kids; YouTube Music; content in player (cards, end screens); email/notifications; background tab; thumbnails <50% visible or <1s; TrueView discovery ads.
- CTR def: "How often viewers watched a video after seeing a thumbnail." / "Impressions click-through rate measures how often viewers watched a video after seeing a registered impression on YouTube."
- "Half of all channels and videos on YouTube have an impressions CTR that can range between 2% and 10%." Wider for new/<100 views. "If a video gets a lot of impressions (such as ... Home Page), it's natural for the CTR to be lower. Videos where most impressions are from ... your channel page may have a higher rate."
- CTR varies "based on the type of content, audience, and where on YouTube the impression was shown".
- Avoid: deciding without enough data; chasing small CTR changes; "Testing several thumbnails or titles on the same video. It's difficult to make sure each video is being seen by the same audience. Differences in CTR might be due to traffic sources".
- Clickbait: "Clickbait videos tend to have low average view duration ... You can tell if your thumbnail is clickbait if it's getting high CTR but low average view duration and lower than expected Impressions."
- Fewer impressions -> higher CTR/AVD because narrower loyal audience.
- Monetization: content not suitable per Community Guidelines can limit impressions; not suitable for advertisers -> limited/no ads.

## Test & Compare — official/first-hand sources on mechanics
- Rene Ritchie, YouTube blog "Rene's Top Five on YouTube: June 14, 2024" https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/renes-top-five-june-14-2024/ (fetched raw) [A-official staff]: "Thumbnail Test & Compare lets creators choose up to 3 thumbnails that YouTube will then split-test among viewers. Each variant will be tested against roughly the same sized audience segment (half for two thumbs, a third for three thumbs), and each viewer will only ever see the same thumbnail variant on the same account... The test will run until the system can determine one of the variants has achieved a significantly higher percentage of watchtime, or 2 weeks go by without a meaningful result." Rolling out to channels with Advanced Features; desktop uploader. WHY watch time: "returns watch time rather than separate metrics on CTR and retention (AVP), because watch time includes both! You have to click to watch and you have to retain to build up time. If you over-index on CTR, it could become click-bait, which could tank retention, and hurt performance. This way, the tool helps build good habits — thumbnails that make a promise and videos that deliver on it!" Tips: "Test immediately to see how your core audience is reacting. When your initial test completes, test again 72+ hours into a video for insights into the wider, more casual audience." "Start testing with bigger differences between thumbnails, like the layouts, different image elements, etc. and ... work your way to more granular differences like clothing colors, background elements, and facial expressions." "Be patient... Not all tests will end with statistically significant results, especially for channels that aren't pulling a ton of impressions". "Orange can pop until everyone does orange, then purple might pop."
- Title A/B (Dec 2025): SEJ (Matt Southern, dated Dec 4 2025) https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-title-a-b-testing-rolls-out-globally-to-creators/562571/ [B secondary quoting Creator Insider]: quote "We want to ensure that your A/B test experiment gets the highest viewer engagement, so we're optimizing for overall watch time over other metrics like CTR..." ; "YouTube distributes impressions as evenly as possible across test variations"; viewer consistently sees the same variant across home feed, watch page, etc.; "watch history and the red progress bar on thumbnails remain the reliable indicators of what you've already watched". ppc.land https://ppc.land/youtube-expands-a-b-testing-to-include-titles-alongside-thumbnails/ dates the announcement Dec 9 2025 (DATE CONFLICT between SEJ Dec 4 and ppc.land Dec 9); ppc.land: 50MB file limit since Oct 2025 (2MB previous); "Winner" = highest "watch time per impression" (ppc/SEJ wording) vs Help Center "watch time share" — YouTube Help never gives the formula; treat both as same-idea, exact formula UNVERIFIED.
- Statistical note: YouTube's own test has a control group excluded; if no winner, FIRST uploaded option becomes default => put the preferred thumbnail FIRST.
- Beaupre (via SEJ https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-bigger-thumbnails-long-form-engagement/588102/ ): desktop homepage redesign w/ larger thumbnails "drove more attention and engagement around long-form" — "We wouldn't have launched that change if it reduced viewership"; "Sometimes choice is overwhelming"; "often less is more"; mobile used to have much smaller thumbnails. ## CTR context — YouTube Help "Decoding CTR & impressions in your Analytics" (answer 16767369) fetched raw 2026-09-30 [A]
- "CTR: How often viewers watched a video after seeing a thumbnail. Tells you how eye-catching your video idea or 'packaging' is." (official use of the word packaging = idea + title/thumbnail appeal).
- "it's important not to view either [impressions or CTR] in isolation, and always consider the context, whether it's traffic sources or audience factors."
- Worked example: 10,000 impressions at 9% CTR (loyal fans) -> 100,000 impressions at 3.5% = "a sign of success".
- Search: fewer impressions, higher CTR (high intent). Homepage: "high volume of impressions but often a lower CTR" (discovery mode, competitive).
- "Early high CTR (first few hours) often comes from your most loyal core audience... the CTR is inflated".
- Niche topic ("a unique historical event"): high CTR/AVD from small pool -> low impressions.

## Policy summary (all fetched raw 2026-09-30) [A]
- Thumbnails policy https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9229980 : don't post thumbnail showing "Violent imagery that intends to shock or disgust", "Graphic or disturbing imagery with blood or gore", "A thumbnail that misleads viewers to think they're about to view something that's not in the video". Age-restrict/remove (no strike) considers "Whether violent or gory imagery is the focal point of the thumbnail"; "Whether the title, description, tags... indicate an intent to shock or disgust". Exceptions for EDSA context. Penalty: removal + possible strike; first violation usually warning; 3 strikes/90 days -> termination.
- Violent/graphic policy https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2802008 : applies to thumbnails; "we do not remove dramatized violence when ... apparent from the content itself, such as with animated content or video games"; "We make a distinction between dramatized violence featuring real human actors and content featuring animations"; road accident imagery "with the intent to shock or disgust" not allowed; natural disasters etc may be allowed/age-restricted with context; educational context must be in images/audio of the video itself (title/description insufficient).
- Age-restriction https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2802167 : applies to custom thumbnails; age-restricted videos not viewable by <18 or signed out; can still monetize but may get limited/no ads.
- Spam/deceptive https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973 : "Malicious clickbait: Using maliciously misleading titles, thumbnails, descriptions, or imagery to trick users into clicking on a video that does not deliver what was promised." -> can suspend monetization/terminate channel.
- Advertiser-friendly https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278 : "Our policies apply to all portions of your content (video, Short, or live stream, thumbnail, title, description, and tags)." Violence tiers: FULL ads: "Severe property damage where death or severe bodily harm likely occurred (such as bombings, fires, building collapses, etc)"; "Coverage of tragedies involving one or more deaths (excluding sensitive events...) with limited or no display of violent acts or their results"; "Implied moment of death or severe bodily harm in an educational, documentary..." ; LIMITED ads: "Footage of disasters that involve visible harm to people or their resulting suffering, such as extreme emotional distress"; "Dramatized violence that results in serious injury where the aftermath or impact is visible"; "Highly graphic descriptions of tragedies"; "Graphic game violence in the thumbnail"; NO ads: "Focus on blood, guts, gore ... crime scenes or accident photos with little to no context"; "Heavy display of blood or gore (such as open wounds like amputated leg or severe burns)"; "Severe agony". Sensitive events (natural disasters, civil emergencies, etc.): "Discussions involving the loss of life or tragedy that are not exploitative or dismissive" can earn ads; NO ads if "profits from or exploits a sensitive event", e.g. "Using keywords related to a sensitive event to attempt to drive additional traffic." Profanity in thumbnail: moderate (e.g. 'shit') = limited; stronger = limited/none.
- Harassment https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2802268 : not allowed: "Content reveling in or mocking the death or serious injury of an identifiable individual."; "Content that realistically simulates deceased or abused individuals describing their death or violence experienced."; Impersonation incl. AI likeness; trademark holder rights enforced.
- YPP "inauthentic content" https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392 : (July 15 2025 rename from "repetitious content": "content that is repetitive or mass-produced") reviewers check "Video metadata (including titles, thumbnails, and descriptions)"; not allowed: "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production"; allowed: "Same intro and outro..., but the bulk of your content is different"; "using AI to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented"; "using AI to edit your video scripts or generate a unique background visual". Not allowed: "Content that repeatedly uses disturbing themes (such as violence or loss) without building a cohesive narrative"; "realistic visuals tricking viewers into believing a fake ... natural disaster has occurred".
- GenAI disclosure https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491 : must disclose realistic altered/synthetic content: makes real person say/do something; alters footage of real event/place; "Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur" (example: "a realistic depiction of a tornado ... moving toward a real city that didn't actually happen"). NOT required: "non-realistic content" (e.g. "AI-generated or altered animation of a missile in a fully animated video"), and "Production assistance, like using generative AI tools to create or improve a video outline, script, thumbnail, title, or infographic", "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs". Labels for non-photorealistic/animated content may appear only in expanded description; auto-labels applied for content made with YouTube's GenAI tools, C2PA metadata, or detected AI; disclosure "won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money"; habitual non-disclosure -> label/penalties incl. YPP suspension. May 27 2026 YouTube blog https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/ moved label for "photorealistic and meaningfully AI altered or generated content" to a more prominent position (below player long-form; overlay on Shorts per 3rd-party summaries) — non-photorealistic/animated stays in expanded description (per Mobilesyrup/TechCrunch summaries; blog body fetch truncated -> partly UNVERIFIED). Thumbnails are not mentioned in disclosure rules except as 'production assistance' = no disclosure.
